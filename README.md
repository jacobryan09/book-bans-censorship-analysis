# Knowledge at Risk
### Censorship Challenges, Outcomes, and Patterns Across Media

A collaborative data science research project examining censorship
challenges and outcomes across books, graphic novels, films, and music.

This project uses Python, exploratory data analysis, and predictive
modeling to investigate the factors associated with censorship outcomes
in the United States.

The research combines two datasets: the Cases dataset, containing
5,369 reported censorship challenges, and the Titles dataset,
containing 84 balanced records across four media categories.

The goal is to understand how censorship challenges begin, what
factors are associated with removal decisions, and how censorship
reasons differ across media.

This section examines 5,369 reported censorship challenges
between 2016 and 2025.

Of these records, 2,554 contain documented decisions, allowing

### Factors Associated With Removal

Predictive modeling achieved approximately 79% accuracy in
classifying removal outcomes.

The analysis identified location as the strongest predictive
signal, with removal rates differing substantially between
school libraries and public libraries.

### Research Limitations

The findings reflect reported censorship challenges with
documented outcomes. Predictive relationships do not
necessarily establish causation.

<img width="3300" height="2550" alt="slide_09" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/19f8b838-4fd1-4e06-91bc-1248a5490dc3" />

### Cross-Media Censorship Analysis

To compare censorship reasons across different media,
the Titles dataset was standardized into a balanced sample
of 84 records, consisting of 21 records per media category.

This approach reduces the influence of unequal sample sizes
when comparing books, graphic novels, films, and music.

<img width="3300" height="2550" alt="slide_10" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/04839b95-a358-4623-bf4b-14da60b32d42" />

### Censorship Reasons Across Media

Exploratory data analysis reveals differences in recorded
censorship reasons across media.

Books and graphic novels are frequently associated with
sexual content and LGBTQIA+ themes, while films and music
show different patterns involving religion, political or
social identity, language, and other reasons.

<img width="3300" height="2550" alt="slide_11" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/d50462d1-4513-4774-a2be-c1a0a6ab7d4c" />

### Cross-Media Comparison

This analysis compares censorship reasons across media
using percentage-point differences.

The findings highlight how recorded censorship concerns
vary depending on the type of material being challenged.

### Research Findings and Recommendations

The combined findings suggest that censorship outcomes
are strongly associated with institutional location,
while recorded censorship reasons vary across media.

The research proposes a standardized database connecting
censorship challenges, outcomes, affected works, and
media identifiers.

<img width="3300" height="2550" alt="slide_13" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/ecb2909c-ac4b-41a2-8c99-16fcf0cda424" />

### Cross-Media Censorship Finder

The project also demonstrates a prototype application

censorship reasons.

This approach illustrates how standardized censorship
